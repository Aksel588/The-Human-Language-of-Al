# Glossary & Author Note

**Conceptual Lexicon, Acknowledgements & Colophon**

> **PDF Version:** [`glossary.pdf`](./glossary.pdf) (Pages 75–77 of *The Human Language of AI*)

---

A reference lexicon of technical architectures, cognitive traps, and craftsmanship principles explored throughout this volume.

### Analog Sanctuaries

Dedicated physical spaces, daily rituals, or deep-work intervals preserved entirely free from digital screens, automated synthesis, and algorithmic feeds.

### API (Application Programming Interface)

A structured protocol allowing external software systems to programmatically request inferences, retrieve data, or execute tool calls.

### Authorship

The indivisible human decision to take moral, intellectual, and legal accountability for a work, regardless of whether a machine assisted in drafting it.

### Bias (Algorithmic & Data)

Systematic skews in training corpora and model weights that reflect historical prejudices, corporate incentives, or uneven cultural representation.

### Closed-Book Test

A pedagogical and diagnostic discipline where a learner steps away from all AI tools and notes to solve a problem unaided, proving genuine competence.

### Cognitive Deskilling (Atrophy)

The gradual erosion of foundational human reasoning, drafting, and diagnostic abilities caused by chronic over-reliance on automated tools.

### Context Window

The finite buffer of tokens an LLM can hold in active memory during inference. Details falling outside this window are functionally forgotten.

### Dialectical Sparring

Engaging with an AI system not as an unquestioned oracle, but as an adversarial thinking partner to cross-examine hypotheses and uncover blind spots.

### Diagnostic Skepticism

An engineering mindset that treats every automated completion as an unverified conjecture requiring empirical validation and edge-case testing.

### Embeddings

High-dimensional mathematical vector representations of text where geometric distance reflects conceptual similarity, powering semantic search and retrieval.

### Epistemic Vigilance

The active cognitive habit of scrutinizing claims, auditing primary sources, and resisting the deceptive persuasion of fluent machine syntax.

### ETL (Extract, Transform, Load)

The systematic data engineering pipeline that extracts raw information, cleans and normalizes it, and prepares it for ingestion.

### Few-Shot Prompting

Providing a model with concrete input-output demonstration exemplars directly inside the prompt context to guide reasoning trajectories.

### Generation

The algorithmic process of calculating conditional probability distributions over token vocabularies to produce synthetic linguistic continuations.

### Hallucination

A phenomenon where a neural network generates factually erroneous, ungrounded, or fabricated assertions with unwavering linguistic confidence.

### Helpfulness Tuning

Alignment design choices that make models agreeable, conversational, and polite— often at the expense of critical friction and truth-telling.

### Illusion of Competence

The cognitive bias where following an AI tutor’s frictionless guidance creates a sensation of mastery without durable independent retention.

### Machine Learning

Statistical systems that parameterize patterns from training distributions to optimize predictive objectives, rather than executing hand-coded rules.

### Maintainer’s Standard

The foundational rule of software craftsmanship: an engineer must never ship or approve code they lack the competence to diagnose, debug, and rewrite unaided under pressure.

### Model Collapse

The degenerative degradation that occurs when successive generations of generative models are recursively trained on synthetic data produced by earlier models.

### Narrow AI vs. AGI

Specialized domain optimization systems (Narrow AI) contrasted with hypothetical autonomous agents possessing general, transferable human-level cognition (AGI).

### Overfitting

When a statistical model memorizes quirks of its training set rather than generalizable principles; mirrored in humans who accumulate notes without understanding.

### Phronesis (Practical Wisdom)

Aristotelian virtue describing ethical discernment, contextual judgment, and prudent decision-making under uncertainty that cannot be codified into algorithms.

### Prompt & Prompt Engineering

The disciplined craft of formulating precise contexts, role constraints, and adversarial specifications to elicit rigorous output from a model.

### Prompt Injection

A security vulnerability where adversarial inputs hijack a model’s system instructions, causing it to bypass safeguards or execute unintended logic.

### RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

An architecture that retrieves relevant verified documents from an external store before synthesizing an answer grounded in external fact.

### Red-Pen Filter (The Author’s Filter)

A rigorous editorial practice where human writers ruthlessly purge robotic transitions, verify facts, and inject authentic personal voice into AI drafts.

### RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)

The post-training alignment method where human evaluators score model completions to steer behavior toward preference-aligned responses.

### Stochastic Parrots

A foundational metaphor highlighting that language models assemble plausible token sequences based on statistical patterns without comprehension, belief, or intent.

### Sycophancy (Model Flattery)

The tendency of alignment-tuned language models to mirror the user’s opinions, validate false assumptions, and flatter biases rather than challenge them.

### Techne (Technical Craft)

Classical Greek term for specialized procedural skill, technical proficiency, and instrumental mastery of tools.

### Temperature & Sampling

Hyperparameters that calibrate randomness during generation: lower values yield deterministic precision, higher values increase vocabulary variance.

### Token

The fundamental discrete unit of text (roughly three-quarters of an English word) processed, embedded, and predicted by language models.

### Training Cutoff

The temporal limit of an LLM’s frozen dataset, after which real-world developments, documentation, and events are unknown without external retrieval.

### Verification

The non-negotiable human duty of independently testing, compiling, or cross-referencing model assertions against primary empirical sources before deployment.
